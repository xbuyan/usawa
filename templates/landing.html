Every finding traces back to a real standard

Most DEI dashboards hand you a 0–100 score with no explanation of what "good" means. Usawa scores the metrics that have a real legal test against that test, and is explicit about the ones that don't.

Four-fifths rule
29 CFR 1607.4(D), EEOC
Hiring funnel and promotion equity are scored against this standard: a selection rate below 80% of the highest group's rate is treated as evidence of adverse impact. This is the same screening test the EEOC itself applies.
Pay equity thresholds
Common consultancy practice
There's no single legal cutoff for pay gap percentage. Usawa uses thresholds common among pay equity consultancies — an unexplained gap of roughly 5% as the point worth investigating — and says so plainly, rather than dressing it up as a legal finding.
Representation pipeline
Descriptive analysis, not a legal test
"Leaky pipeline" drop-off across levels has no regulatory standard. It's useful for spotting where representation erodes — Usawa presents it as a signal to investigate, never as compliance evidence.
